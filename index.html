Filter coffee, the way Chennai taught us.

Single-origin beans from Chikmagalur, Coorg and Araku Valley, cut with roasted chicory in the old 70:30 ratio, decocted slow overnight and poured high enough to froth. Since 1998, off Devaraja Mudali Street.

The pour, step by step

Rukmini Iyer opened the shop in 1998 with one filter and ten steel chairs. The chairs multiplied; the method didn't move an inch.

1

Roast

Small batches, roasted just past second crack — dark enough to stand up to chicory, not so dark the estate disappears.

2

Grind

Ground to order. Coffee held ground overnight loses half its smell to the air by morning.

3

Decoct

Packed into the filter and left to drip for six to eight hours — no plunger, no pressure, just gravity and time.

4

Pour

Two parts hot milk to one part decoction, poured high between tumbler and davara until it froths.

As the story goes, that froth is where "degree coffee" got its name — bar owners once checked their milk with a lactometer marked in degrees, and a good hard froth meant nobody had watered it down.
